Why it is one work

Every module writes into the same frame

The modules were built by different people with different tools, from a latent-space wind surrogate on an RTX 5090 to a Blender export of a station plate. What makes them one system is a strict contract: one 4 m raster with a fixed origin and orientation, one metre-based glTF world, and a published transform between the two. Anything that lands in that frame shows up in the viewer without preprocessing.

core008 city model and its 1 m voxelisation

The base asset is a full-city glTF with buildings, roads, footways, grass and trees, Y up in metres. For the physics it is voxelised at 1 m over a 64 m tall domain of 2816 × 3072 cells and conservatively coarsened to 4 m: a 4 m cell counts as solid if any of its 1 m voxels does, so narrow streets close and the wind field never leaks through a wall. The viewer confirms the alignment by matching the model's building bounding box against the voxel extent to the metre.

306 missing buildings from OpenStreetMap

An audit compared every mapped building inside the ground rectangle against the model. Of 6,631 features, 309 conservative candidates lay wholly inside the bounds with under 2 % overlap against existing buildings, and 306 also cleared the drivable lanes. They were extruded in Blender with estimated heights and procedural facades, registered in the same EPSG:32630 frame (median residual 23 mm on 5,838 control points), and exported as a separate GLB so the original model stays byte-identical.

  • Three candidates with real road conflicts were deliberately left out and are listed in the status report.
  • Heights, roof shapes and windows are estimates, not photogrammetry. No satellite pixels were traced.
  • The physics fields were not recomputed after the additions. They describe the original geometry.

South Kensington station detail tile

A Blender-authored plate of the station district: 245 OSM buildings with PBR facades, the station arcade, the open railway cutting and platforms, gardens, trees and street lamps. Its local frame is rotated against the main model by the UTM grid convergence, so it is placed with a rigid transform fitted on the 237 buildings the two files share, matched by OSM id. Inside the plate the main model's ground, roads and duplicate buildings are clipped away; outside it the tile is clipped. It stays off by default because its palette reads differently from the base model.

Wind: a latent U-Net stepped over the whole city

The published SCALED surrogate was trained on a 1024 m South Kensington case. To run it over the 2.8 km domain the geometry is encoded once into a latent field, then each time step advances nine 1024 m latent blocks with a 4-cell halo and never returns to physical space until the final decode. That is the same latent-to-latent regime the model was trained in, and it removed the momentum loss that a physical-space blocking attempt showed. From rest the field settles into a statistically steady, turbulent state after about 50 steps with the inflow from the west.

Temperature: two models, one honest comparison

The 3-D experiment drives a controlled heat solver with wind steps 82 to 100 (ambient 26 °C, ground and roofs at 30 °C) and asks a single-step U-Net to predict each next state. Used one step at a time it beats the persistence baseline from 180 s onward and stays near 0.26 °C error. Fed its own output recursively, its error grows linearly to 1.5 °C, so the recursive mode is reported as not usable. The 2-D label model is the one shown in the viewer: a fast surface-layer solver on the same wind, with building footprints, roof heights and vegetation as labels, giving a 1500 s summer-afternoon evolution in 7 s of compute.

Sunlight: shadows and sky view as a visibility transport problem

The same idea as the wind and flood solvers, applied to light: a cell is in shadow when the height field along the ray towards the sun rises above the cell's own line of sight, which is a running maximum along that ray. The running maximum is built by doubling (shift, take the maximum, shift twice as far, repeat), so a shadow of any length costs only log₂ of its length in layers, and every shift is an exact integer step with no interpolation. The same network run at 16 azimuths and 18 altitudes gives each cell its horizon angles and hence its sky-view factor. An ASHRAE clear-sky model then turns sun position, shadow and sky view into irradiance. Both solstice days at 1 m over the whole 2.8 × 2.6 km domain take under two seconds on the GPU.

Interpretation boundaryClear sky only, with monthly ASHRAE coefficients; London's real annual irradiation is roughly half the clear-sky value. Buildings are flat-topped columns, so arcades and overhangs are filled and facades receive no irradiance. Trees are not in the height field and only attenuate the beam on their own canopy cells. One ground bounce, no inter-reflections.

Coupling the sun into the 3-D temperature model

The 3-D model solves a surface energy balance and 3-D advection–diffusion, but its shortwave input was one irradiance number for the whole city with an in-house per-building shadow loop, and one longwave value from the sky. The coupling is one-way: the shadow term becomes the solar model's sunlit fraction and sky-view diffuse light, and the downwelling longwave is weighted by the sky-view factor with walls radiating at air temperature. Everything else in the model, including latent heat, storage, anthropogenic heat and the solver, is untouched. It is also the first time this model runs on the full core008 domain, using a 3-D velocity cache built from the SCALED wind.

The differences sit where the sky view is low: street canyons, courtyards and the north sides of buildings lose diffuse and longwave income and come out 1–1.5 °C cooler at the surface, locally up to 6 °C, while open ground and parks barely move. Over the summer-solstice day the hourly chain shows the city surface running about 9 °C above the air by mid-afternoon and 1–2.5 °C below it at night, a near-surface air excess peaking at +2.0 °C around 13:00–14:00 and turning to −0.8 °C at night, and vegetation surfaces staying below the air all day.

Interpretation boundaryNo observations. The model's scenario parameters set the absolute temperatures; the coupling only redistributes shortwave and longwave in space. The surface balance is instantaneous with no ground heat storage, so night cooling is too fast. Cloud is a simple direct-beam reduction, the wind is the steady SCALED field cycled with no thermal feedback, and facades carry no radiation term.

Pollutant transport carried by the wind steps

A transport solver runs on the 4 m wind field with each 25 s wind step split into fifty 0.5 s sub-steps and the wind interpolated between frames. The source is a continuous line along the upwind western edge, so the plume crosses the whole city. By step 100 the domain holds 17.8 million units of the 18 million emitted; outflow has only just begun. Hot spots form at the windward feet of large buildings where the wind converges, an artefact of the wind field rather than of the transport scheme.

ScopeRun at 4 m only. The 1 m grid would be 550 million cells and exceeds GPU memory. The source strength and boundary velocities are experimental settings, not an emissions inventory.

Surface-water flooding from a cloudburst

The discretised shallow-water equations are expressed as fixed convolution stencils on the GPU, following the Advances in Water Resources paper, with three changes for a city: an Arakawa C grid so buildings act as walls and dry cells stay still, a conjugate-gradient free-surface solve for controllable mass conservation, and first-order upwind advection. Rain falls on a 1 m bare-earth LIDAR terrain with buildings raised as solid blocks, sewers remove 12 mm/h from paved ground and roofs, green areas infiltrate 20 mm/h, and excess roof runoff drops to the nearest ground cell.

Solver verification covers a still lake with buildings (zero motion), a closed basin under rain (volume error 1.8e-8), Manning uniform flow (depth 0.18488 m against the same analytic value), and Stoker dam breaks, where the non-conservative momentum form is 0.7 % off for a mild jump but does not converge for a strong one. Urban depths are centimetres to decimetres, so that limit does not bite here.

Interpretation boundaryThere are no observations of the 2021 event on this geometry. Rainfall, sewer capacity, infiltration and roughness are set values, and there is no pipe network. Real pits in the DTM (the railway cutting at South Kensington station, basements, light wells) collect most of the deepest water, so read the percentile and area metrics rather than the maximum.

SUMO traffic on a repaired OSM network

The first-version network left most of the city unreachable: its largest strongly connected component had 232 edges. Revision 02 re-qualified the lanes geometrically on the original OSM network, reopening 611 lanes and restoring 21 missing middle segments, and closed 65 short lanes on building-clearance evidence. Every one of the 851 signal heads reads the recorded state of its own connection. The result is a one-hour run that passes six release gates: integrity, identity, no teleports, on-drawn-road, signals, and native replay.

A pigeon flock over South Kensington station

One hundred birds run for an hour with a roost on the station roof and forage sites within 90 m. The geometry they avoid is the same 4 m building mask the wind field uses, cropped to the station district, which is the clearest small example of the shared frame: the flock file records the crop origin and spacing, and the viewer places it without any calibration. At the noon clock the model stays in its murmuration state for the whole hour, so no roosting or foraging trips appear. The bird tracker in the viewer follows the flock centroid or a single bird.

Integration

The viewer

A single three.js page reads every layer straight from the files on disk. Field frames are fetched by HTTP Range request from the float16 arrays and decoded in the browser, so a new run appears the moment its arrays are dropped into the folder. Nothing is tiled, converted or cached in advance.

How the page is put together

  • One loop, two halves. A campus tour (overview, junction, traffic map, UAVs, birds) then an overhead pass that plays wind, temperature, sunlight, the day cycle, pollution and flooding one after another. Big tabs jump to any view; auto loop off holds the current one.
  • Field playback. Sequential mode shows one field at a time at 12 steps per second; the flood, the hourly shadows and the day cycle run at 2 frames per second on their own clocks. Overlay mode lets layers be combined by hand.
  • 1 m without preprocessing. The hourly shadow frames are 8.7 MB each and are expanded in the browser into a 3072 × 2816 texture draped over the streets, so the 1 m result is shown at full resolution straight from the array.
  • Replay modules reused unchanged. The traffic, UAV, signal and bird modules from the demo package are wrapped by replay.js rather than rewritten, so the colleague's package remains the source of truth.
  • Decoupled clocks. The replay clock runs the full hour for UAVs and birds while the 300 s traffic sample loops inside it until the full-hour files are dropped in.
  • Deployment. Served from the workstation and published through a Tailscale Funnel; remote viewers see roughly 20 Mbps, so the fields section can stutter until frames are pre-rendered to small textures, which is the next step.
Read before citing

Claim boundary

Every module keeps its own limitations next to its results. The short version, per layer:

  • GeometryAdded buildings and the station tile are estimates registered to the frame, not surveys. Physics fields were not recomputed after the 306 additions.
  • WindTransfer of a surrogate trained on a 1024 m case to new geometry, with no CFD or measurement reference. Time scale inherited, domain capped at 64 m.
  • TemperatureExperimental heat scenario against an adapted controlled solver. The recursive 3-D mode is reported as unusable; the 2-D model is a label model, not a heat budget. The solar-coupled day cycle has no observations and no ground heat storage.
  • SunlightClear sky only; real irradiation is roughly half. Flat-topped building columns, no facade irradiance, trees only attenuate, one ground bounce.
  • PollutionIdealised continuous line source with set strength. 4 m only. Hot spots inherit the wind field's convergence.
  • FloodingNo observed inundation for this geometry. Drainage is a uniform rate with no pipe network; DTM pits dominate the maxima.
  • AgentsRule-based traffic with simulated signal timing, not TfL data. Deliveries replay an earlier planner run. Birds are a behavioural model with no observation.
Reproduce

Run it

The viewer needs only the system Python. The large assets are kept out of git because of GitHub's file limits: the two city GLBs, the field arrays and the full-hour traffic replay are synced separately and placed as listed below.

Start the viewer

Static server with Range support; opens the 3-D page on the next free port.

cd UrbanWorldModelVisualizer
python3 serve.py        # localhost:8787/viewer/3d/
python3 serve.py 9000   # custom port
