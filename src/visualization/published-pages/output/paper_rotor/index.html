Stage one · Rotor model port and numerical checks

The paper rotor model and our MAC solver

A physics solver implemented in PyTorch with fixed Conv3d operators and the paper's weighted actuator-disk force model. It has no trained weights and is not a learned physics surrogate.

Scope: These are computed, small single-phase test cases, not the original wind farm or a reproduction of the complete OpenFOAM model. Two-phase waves, SST turbulence, overset grids, floating-body six-degree-of-freedom motion and moorings are not implemented. Original OpenFOAM results are unavailable as a numerical benchmark.

Paper: Davidson et al. — Turbines and thrusters (2026) . Full implementation details: README。

Why does platform motion change thrust?

The paper subtracts hub velocity from the sampled local wind velocity. The figure compares analytical loads at a prescribed local wind speed, not simulated platform motion: downwind hub motion reduces relative wind speed and thrust decreases with its square.

For Ct=0.75, the paper's conversion gives Ct′=4/3, matching our original stationary-turbine formula. The additions are spatial weighting, relative hub velocity, and forces and moments transferred to the platform; blade rotation is not resolved.

What remains for a complete reproduction?

The next stage must match the wind-tunnel boundaries, turbulent inlet and wall settings in Appendix B.2 and validate against the authors' OpenFOAM cases or traceable reference data. Only then can the study extend to two-phase flow, free surfaces, platform motion and moorings. These results do not support claims of outperforming OpenFOAM or predicting electrical power.

Results were computed on an RTX 5090. TF32 is disabled for the fixed convolutional pressure operator to preserve the numerical precision of the divergence constraint.
